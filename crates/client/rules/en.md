# Snook Legend: the rules of eight-ball, shown in the game.

## The game

### A regional Brazilian rule
Snook Legend plays a regional Brazilian eight-ball rule, the one of bars and pool halls, with penalty balls. It is not the official tournament rule (the WPA's or a federation's): if you know the official rule, read the fouls and the 8-ball below, because they differ a lot.

### The aim
Each player has a group of seven balls: solids (1 to 7) or stripes (9 to 15). Pocket all of yours and then the 8-ball, before your opponent does.

### Your turn
You keep shooting as long as you pocket a ball of your group without a foul. If you miss, foul or pocket one of your opponent's balls, the turn passes.

## The break

### The break shot
The cue ball starts behind the head string. There is no illegal break: no ball has to go down or reach a cushion.

### The 8-ball on the break
Pocketing only the 8 on the break wins the game on the spot. Pocketing the 8 together with any other ball (the cue ball or any other) loses the game.

### After the break
If you pocket a ball on the break without a foul, the first ball that went down decides your group and you shoot again.

### Open table
Until the groups are decided the table is open: you may hit any ball first except the 8. The first ball you pocket on a shot without a foul gives you its group, and your opponent the other. On an open table there is no opponent's ball yet: a foul costs only the turn.

## Fouls and penalties

### Penalty balls
Every foul passes the turn, and most of them give your opponent balls: the lowest numbers of their group still on the table come off it, as if they had pocketed them. Penalties add up. The 8-ball never comes off as a penalty: an opponent left with no balls is on the 8.

### Missing the ball
Hitting no ball at all: one ball to your opponent. Missing and pocketing the cue ball as well: two balls.

### Hitting your opponent's ball
Hitting a ball that isn't of your group first, directly: two balls to your opponent. If the cue ball went to a cushion first: one ball.

### Pocketing the cue ball
One ball to your opponent, who gets the cue ball in hand and may place it anywhere on the table (behind the head string after a break). Only a cue ball that went down or left the table is in hand; after any other foul it is played from where it stopped.

### Balls off the table
The cue ball off the table: you lose the turn and your opponent gets it in hand. A ball of your group off the table comes back to the table, and you lose the turn. One of your opponent's balls off the table is out (it counts for them), and you lose the turn.

### Your opponent's ball
Pocketing one of your opponent's balls passes the turn, even if one of yours went down too. It stays down, for them.

### The shot clock
When a game has a shot clock and it runs out, the turn passes.

## The 8-ball

### Calling the pocket
When your group is cleared, choose a pocket for the 8 before you shoot. Pocket only the 8 there, without a foul, and you win.

### Losing on the 8
You lose if you pocket the 8 before your group is cleared, in a pocket other than the one you called, together with the cue ball or one of your opponent's balls, on a foul, or if you knock it off the table.
