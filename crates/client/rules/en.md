## The game

### A regional Brazilian rule
Snook Legend plays a regional Brazilian eight-ball rule, the one of bars and pool halls, with penalty balls. It is not the official tournament rule (the WPA's or a federation's): if you know the official rule, read the fouls and the 8-ball below, because they differ a lot.

### The aim
Each player has a group of seven balls: solids (1 to 7) or stripes (9 to 15). Pocket all of yours and then the 8-ball, before your opponent does.

### Your turn
You keep shooting as long as you pocket a ball of your group without a foul. If you miss, foul or pocket one of your opponent's balls, the turn passes.

## The break

### The break shot
On the break, and only then, the cue ball is in hand: you may place it anywhere behind the head string, even against a cushion, but never in a pocket's mouth. No ball has to go down or reach a cushion, but the cue ball must hit a ball (see below).

### The 8-ball on the break
Pocketing only the 8 on the break wins the game on the spot. Pocketing the 8 together with any other ball (the cue ball or any other) loses the game. If the 8 leaves the table on the break, it goes back on the table where the front ball of the rack stood (or as near as there is room) and you lose the turn: it's a foul.

### Only the cue ball on the break
If the cue ball goes down or leaves the table on the break and no ball is made, you lose the turn and the break passes to your opponent. The balls stay where they stopped, and they place the cue ball anywhere behind the head string, as you did: their shot counts as a break. The same goes if the cue ball goes down with balls of both groups (a solid and a stripe, in any order): the break passes to your opponent, with the cue ball in hand behind the head string and no penalty ball; the balls made stay down and the table stays open. If one ball (or balls of one group only) goes down along with the cue ball, the first one down still decides the groups, and it's an ordinary foul: your opponent's lowest ball comes off, the cue ball goes back to the head spot and they play from there, without ball in hand.

### A break that hits no ball
If the break hits no ball, you lose the turn and the break passes to your opponent: the balls are still racked, and they place the cue ball anywhere behind the head string.

### After the break
On the break, the first ball that goes down decides your group, even if the cue ball goes down too (unless balls of both groups go down with the cue ball: see above). If only balls of your group go down, you shoot again. If one of your opponent's goes down too (the 3 and then the 13, say: you take the solids), it stays down, for them, and the turn passes.

### Open table
Until the groups are decided the table is open: you may hit any ball first except the 8. The first ball you pocket on a shot without a foul gives you its group, and your opponent the other. On an open table there is no opponent's ball yet: a foul costs only the turn. If no ball of either group is left on an open table, both players are on the 8.

## Fouls and penalties

### Penalty balls
Every foul passes the turn, and most of them give your opponent balls: the lowest numbers of their group still on the table come off it, as if they had pocketed them. Penalties add up. The 8-ball never comes off as a penalty: an opponent left with no balls is on the 8.

### Missing the ball
Hitting none of your balls: your opponent's lowest ball on the table comes off (it counts for them), and you lose the turn. Hitting the 8 first before your group is cleared costs the same. Missing and pocketing the cue ball as well: your opponent's two lowest balls.

### Hitting your opponent's ball
Hitting one of your opponent's balls first, directly: their two lowest balls come off, and you lose the turn. If the ball you hit goes down, it stays down and the two come off all the same. If the cue ball went to a cushion first: one ball. Hitting your opponent's ball directly and pocketing the cue ball as well: their three lowest.

### Pocketing the cue ball
One ball to your opponent, and the cue ball goes back to the head spot, where the break is played from (or right behind a ball lying there). There is no ball in hand: your opponent plays from there. The cue ball is in hand on the break only; after any other foul it is played from where it stopped.

### Balls off the table
The cue ball off the table costs the same as pocketing it: your opponent's lowest ball comes off (it counts for them), you lose the turn and the cue ball goes back to the head spot. A ball of your group off the table comes back to the table, and you lose the turn. One of your opponent's balls off the table is out (it counts for them), and you lose the turn.

### Your opponent's ball
Pocketing one of your opponent's balls passes the turn, even if one of yours went down too. It stays down, for them.

### The shot clock
When a game has a shot clock and it runs out, the turn passes.

## The 8-ball

### Calling the pocket
When your group is cleared, choose a pocket for the 8 before you shoot. Pocket only the 8 there, without a foul, and you win. The pocket you call stands from your first shot on the 8 to the end of the game, even if that shot is a safety or a miss: you can't change it.

### Losing on the 8
You lose if you pocket the 8 before your group is cleared, in a pocket other than the one you called, together with the cue ball or one of your opponent's balls, on a foul, or if you knock it off the table after the break.
